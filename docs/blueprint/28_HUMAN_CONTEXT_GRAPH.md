## 1. The Civilizational Pivot

Traditional graphs capture static data:

- Social graphs link who knows whom.
- Knowledge graphs link what concepts mean in the abstract.

The **EKA Human Context Graph** connects meaning to lived human experience:

$$\text{Person} \longleftrightarrow \text{Community} \longleftrightarrow \text{Culture} \longleftrightarrow \text{Place} \longleftrightarrow \text{Tradition} \longleftrightarrow \text{Language} \longleftrightarrow \text{Event} \longleftrightarrow \text{Business} \longleftrightarrow \text{Product} \longleftrightarrow \text{Experience} \longleftrightarrow \text{History}$$

### The Critical Asymmetry

EKA does not merely know _what_ something is. EKA knows:

- **Who** it matters to (Communities, Lineages, Families)
- **Where** it matters (Sacred Rivers, Localities, Historic Centers)
- **Why** it matters (Spiritual Aspirations, Vows, Ancestral Memory)
- **In what context** it is practiced (Solar/Lunar Timings, Fasting Windows, Ritual Purity)


### The Knowledge Identity ("EKA Passport")

Instead of tracking users for intrusive ad auctions, EKA creates an **EKA Cultural & Wisdom Passport**:

- Traditions explored and practices maintained.
- Sacred geographic regions visited and understood in depth.
- Oral histories preserved or verified.
- Circles joined and community contributions made.

Users carry a portable knowledge identity that reflects who they are becoming.
